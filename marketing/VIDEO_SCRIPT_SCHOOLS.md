# Beacon — School Explainer Video
### "Every teacher. Every week. Every term."

**Audience:** school proprietors, headteachers, and directors of studies — the people who
sign, not the people who teach.
**Format:** 90-second primary cut (with 30s and 15s cutdowns below).
**Tone:** cinematic, warm, documentary-real. Confident, never desperate. Ghanaian
throughout — Accra light, real compounds, real chalk dust. No stock-footage American
classrooms, no generic "diverse office" b-roll.

**Core promise:** *Your teachers get their Sundays back. You get proof of what's taught.*

---

## ⚠️ Production note first — read this

AI video models are excellent at **atmosphere, faces, light, and motion** and unreliable at
**legible text and UI**. So:

- **Shoot every screen moment for real.** Record the actual Beacon UI with a screen
  recorder and intercut it. Do not ask a video model to invent a dashboard.
- **Add all on-screen text in the edit** as titles, not in the generation.
- Use AI for the *human* shots — the kitchen table, the compound, the classroom, the office.
- Shoot at 24fps, anamorphic-ish, shallow depth of field, warm practicals. Golden hour and
  blue hour only. Avoid harsh midday sun.

## Practical notes

- **Music:** solo piano opening → add a low string bed at the turn → full warm ensemble at
  the assembly shot → resolve on a single sustained note. Nothing corporate, nothing
  triumphant. Think *dignified relief*, not *product launch*.
- **Colour:** warm throughout — amber skin tones, golden exteriors, navy interiors. Grade
  the "before" slightly cooler and desaturated; warm it up after the turn, so the
  transition is felt before it's understood.
- **Casting:** real Ghanaian teachers and children, not models. Casting authenticity is the
  whole credibility of this film.
- **Length discipline:** if anything has to go, cut from Act 3, never from Shot 11.
- **Subtitles:** burn in English captions — most viewers will watch on a phone with sound
  off.
