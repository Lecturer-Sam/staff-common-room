# Editorial notes for the opening episodes

These notes accompany **KWEEKU SAMPSON: THE TRUMP CARD — Continuation and Conclusion**. The new manuscript picks up at Episode 4, Chapter 5; it does not silently rewrite the supplied opening. Apply the following changes when assembling a unified draft.

## Essential continuity fixes

1. **Settle the protagonist's spelling.** The supplied title says **KWEEKU**, while the prose says **Kweku**. The continuation preserves that distinction rather than making an unrequested change. For a unified edition, use **KWEKU** throughout unless the title spelling is intentional.

2. **Correct the two-thousand-cedi payment.** Episode 4, Chapter 4 has Berlinda hand over “two crisp notes” totalling GH₵2,000. Replace this with a counted bundle of valid denominations appropriate to the story's year. Set the year before finalizing currency details.

3. **Reconcile the first recording inventory.** Episode 1 describes four filmed events—Arafat entering, Berlinda entering, Arafat leaving, and Berlinda leaving—plus an audio recording, but later counts only three video files. Keep four clips and an audio file, or explain which footage was not saved. When Kweku plays them back, identify clips by their contents rather than inconsistent numbering.

4. **Repair the Thursday-to-Friday interval.** Episode 1 moves from Thursday's capture through “three days” of deliberation to a Friday confrontation. Choose a clear interval, such as a week of deliberation followed by the next Friday, and adjust the intervening wording.

5. **Fix the Aburi recording's start point.** Arafat, not Madam Baah, says “Adwoa,” and that line occurs before Kweku starts recording. Episode 2 subsequently treats the name as recorded. Either move the recording's start earlier or remove the claim. The continuation deliberately distinguishes Kweku's recollection from what a file actually contains.

6. **Make the two examination schemes one coherent sequence.** Episode 2 already concerns planned BECE sales; Episode 4 describes the earlier scheme as merely internal school fraud. Revise the latter account to describe a resumed or expanded BECE operation. The continuation establishes that Arafat's promised cancellation and refunds were incomplete, and the network restarted under new arrangements.

7. **Clarify the transaction alias.** Madam Baah should recognize a pupil at her own school. “You're the new one?” can mean a new buyer, not an unknown child. Add a line establishing that she accepts the name supplied by Obeng as a payment identifier. Do not rely on “Kwame Mensah” physically disguising Kweku from a teacher who knows him.

8. **Keep evidence technically plausible.** Establish video recording among the old phone's working capabilities. Distinguish device time or file metadata from a timestamp necessarily embedded in footage. Avoid describing a distant, muffled recording as conclusive proof of everything that happened inside a room. Verification and corroboration matter to the ending.

9. **Check physical geography and access.** Draw a simple plan of the washrooms, alley, kitchen, and tree. A separate staff block across an alley cannot also be the room immediately beyond the boys' shared partition without explanation. In Episode 2, distinguish already-missing window blades from any Kweku removes and subsequently replaces. Verify the town's location and travel routes; do not casually interchange a trotro and a shared taxi.

## Character and narrative framing

10. **Treat Kweku's early conclusions as conclusions, not omniscient facts.** A child checking an exit, appearing composed, or returning to class does not establish consent, lack of trauma, or safety. Revise “calculation, not trauma” into something Kweku initially assumes and later recognizes he cannot know. Use “abuse” or “exploitation,” rather than the narrator's neutral description of an “affair,” for an adult teacher's involvement with a fourteen-year-old.

11. **Separate observation from guilt.** A list, a meeting, a payment, or a grade change can justify concern and investigation without proving every person's knowledge or conduct. The continuation keeps the children's identities private and gives independent evidence and their own accounts a role. This also makes the investigation more credible dramatically.

12. **Avoid defining Abena's ability by a post-abuse grade.** Replace the statement that a C reflects her “actual ability” with a description of performance under ordinary assessment at that time. Her capacity to learn is not fixed by either an inflated grade or a difficult period of recovery.

13. **Keep the early victories provisional.** Small additions can strengthen the bridge to the ending: Kweku cannot verify Arafat's promise; Berlinda's compliance unsettles him once the initial satisfaction passes; accepting favours begins to feel different from receiving respect. Do not erase his pleasure in power. That pleasure is what gives his later choices weight.

## Final publication checks

- **Establish a calendar.** Confirm that 14 March falls on a Saturday in the chosen year and align the sports day, BECE period, phone technology, currency, and school terms. The Saturday district-office visit should be an expressly arranged appointment rather than an assumed normal working day.
- **Check the school model.** Clarify whether references to basic-school “fees” concern supplies, levies, examination costs, or tuition. Make the school bus, sick bay, staffing, and laboratory consistent with the school's resources and history.
- **Commission a Ghanaian context and safeguarding review.** Confirm local terminology, examination procedures, agency responsibilities, child-witness protections, juvenile proceedings, and disciplinary outcomes before publication. The continuation's institutional sequences are fictional storytelling, not a description of a guaranteed legal pathway.
- **Preserve narrative privacy.** The story can name adult wrongdoing without reproducing intimate recordings or making girls disclose their experiences to earn the reader's belief. Berlinda's account is not Kweku's property, and her safety is not conditional on becoming likeable.

## Throughline of the completed ending

Kweku begins by making information a private weapon. He ends by understanding when it must become a responsibly shared record—and when it belongs to someone else. He retains his intelligence, watchfulness, anger, and capacity to act. What changes is his willingness to mistake control for protection.

The cartel, the two abuse cases, his own coercion, and his education receive distinct resolutions. Neither poverty nor institutional failure disappears overnight. The family photograph closes the phone motif by giving the device an ordinary, consensual purpose instead of another target.
